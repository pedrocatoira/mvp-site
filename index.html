From backlog to first release in four steps

No new tool to learn and no copies of your issues. The map is a view of Jira, so it stays true.

  1. Create an MVP

    Give it a name. You start with five steps and three lanes: MVP, v1 and v2. Rename anything by clicking it.

  2. Add issues

    Find them with JQL, from an epic, or by key and text. Tick the ones you want; they wait in the Unplaced tray.

  3. Arrange the map

    Drag each card to its step and lane. Link lanes to Jira releases so fix versions follow the cards.

  4. Track it live

    Cards change colour as work moves. Lane totals and the early-start warning keep the cut honest.

Built for the conversation about what ships first

Live from Jira
Status, story points, flags, epics and assignees update on every open map within seconds. Nobody refreshes, nobody copies.
Releases that follow the map
Link a lane to an unreleased version. Move a card into it and the issue’s fix version follows, without touching any other versions.
Early-start warning
If work starts in v1 while the MVP lane still has open issues, the lane turns orange and the map tells you how many.
Totals at the end of every lane
Issues and story points per status, plus percent done, so you can see whether a lane fits the release.
Edit in place
Rename steps and lanes where they are. Drag cards, steps and lanes. Story points edit right on the card.
Respects Jira permissions
People only see issues Jira lets them see, and changes to issues are made as them, under their own permissions.

Your Jira data stays in Jira

Runs on Atlassian

Built on Atlassian Forge. We run no servers and the app sends nothing outside Atlassian.

Stores ids, not content

The app keeps your map layout and settings. Issue summaries, descriptions and people’s details are read from Jira when shown, never stored.

Data residency

App data lives in Atlassian’s Forge hosted storage, which follows your site’s data residency location.
